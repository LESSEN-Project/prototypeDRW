## 1. De Rode Winkel and the testbed (2 min)

De Rode Winkel is a family-owned clothing shop in Utrecht, founded in 1837, with a web shop and one physical store. Customers ask the shop questions over WhatsApp about sizes, returns, shipping, payment, repairs and opening hours. The shop made those conversations available for research, anonymised, which makes it our case study and data partner. Say in one sentence why that matters: realistic conversational data in a language other than English or Chinese is hard to get, and this is a real customer population writing real Dutch.

The prototype is a Rasa CALM assistant with retrieval-augmented generation over a knowledge base distilled from those conversations. Every model runs on our own hardware through Ollama on one 24 GB GPU: gemma3:4b decides what to do with a message, bge-m3 embeds it, gemma3:12b writes the answer in Dutch and in the customer's register (u or je), or declines when the documents lack the answer. Show the architecture diagram from the README. The prototype is a testbed for research questions, and everything (code, suite, study tooling) is open source under LESSEN. One thing to say plainly before moving on: the two research lines that follow both ask how a technique from the literature behaves on this realistic data.

## 2. The data (2.5 min)

Start from the raw material: 2,112 customer WhatsApp conversations. Three steps turned them into a knowledge base.

Pseudonymisation came first, with discombobulator, a rule-based toolkit that parses chat logs and replaces names, e-mail addresses, phone numbers, street addresses and tracking codes with placeholders. The extraction pipeline only ever saw cleaned text, and this is the step that made sharing the data at all possible. Say that it is rule-based on purpose: a deterministic step you can audit, before any LLM touches the data.

Extraction next: llama3.3:70b read each conversation and wrote at most one question and answer, constrained to a fixed list of ten categories (maatadvies, retourneren, verzending, betalen, ...). The fixed list was a fix for fragmentation: a first run without it produced dozens of near-duplicate categories. Roughly a third of conversations yield nothing usable (small talk, order status, a photo), so in the 100-conversation pilot about 65 Q&As came out.

Clustering last: the extracted questions were embedded, clustered by agglomerative clustering on cosine distance, and each cluster was synthesised into one archetype Q&A by the LLM. The result is 97 Dutch Q&A files in 13 categories; the assortment category was written afterwards by hand to cover "verkopen jullie ook ...?" questions. One retrieval detail worth ten seconds: every Q&A lives in its own file, because Rasa chunks at 1,000 characters and several questions in one chunk gave one diluted vector. The one-file layout plus bge-m3 is what made retrieval robust to colloquial rewordings ("m'n rits is kapot" finds the repair document).

If someone asks about privacy after the talk: the answers still quote the shop's own phone number, e-mail and address, which are public, and a residual check of the extracted answers for personal data is on the list before any release of the corpus.

## 3. Contrastive examples: a null result (4.5 min)

The question, in one breath. Yazan, Verberne and Situmeang (ECIR 2025, doi:10.1007/978-3-031-88714-7_40) propose contrastive examples for retrieval-augmented generation. Our version: does showing the model documents labelled as irrelevant, next to the retrieved ones, make a small model decline questions whose specific fact is missing from the knowledge base? The failure this targets is the near-miss: "Mag mijn hond mee de winkel in?" retrieves the store document, which says nothing about dogs, and the model answers anyway.

The setup, in one breath. A 124-case suite with three strata: 68 answerable, 40 near-miss (right topic, fact absent), 16 out-of-scope ("Hoe laat is het nu?"). Ten answer models, k = 0 to 4 contrast documents in three selection modes, five repeats at temperature 0, 380 runs, llama3.3:70b as groundedness judge with gpt-oss:20b as agreement check.

First slide, the baseline regimes at k = 0. Show the full table or the six rows below; the point is the three regimes.

| answer model | A false-abstain | A grounded | N false-answer | O false-answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3.5:27b | 2.9 | 80.9 | 10.0 | 0.0 |
| gemma3:12b | 8.8 | 76.5 | 17.5 | 0.0 |
| mistral-small3.2 | 13.2 | 80.9 | 2.5 | 0.0 |
| llama3.3:70b | 0.0 | 76.5 | 40.0 (52.5 hedged) | 18.8 |
| gemma3:4b | 0.0 | 72.1 | 92.5 | 81.2 |
| GEITje-7B-ultra | 1.5 | 48.5 | 95.0 | 100.0 |

Regime one follows the abstention instruction and trades false abstentions against false answers along one axis. Regime two (llama3.3:70b, qwen3.5:9b) rarely emits the abstention signal and instead writes prose saying the information is missing, which the customer experiences as correct and the fallback flow never sees. Regime three answers everything, including questions about the time of day, and the three Dutch-tuned models are all in it. The model is the lever.

Second slide, what contrast documents do. No contrast condition on any model lowers the near-miss false-answer rate significantly against that model's own baseline. The one significant change goes the other way: gemma3:12b with four next-ranked contrast documents rises from 17.5% to 32.5% (McNemar p = 0.03, six cases moved, none back). The effect that does hold is a threshold shift; pooled over modes on gemma3:12b:

| k | A answered | N answered | O answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 91.2 | 24.5 | 2.5 |
| 2 | 96.7 | 23.8 | 12.5 |
| 4 | 99.1 | 31.7 | 23.8 |

Contrast documents act as more context and lower the bar for answering on every stratum. Grounding on answerable questions does not change. Two cheaper baselines, in one sentence each: passing two documents in place of four moves the model in the same direction, towards answering; a similarity floor on the retrieval distance separates out-of-scope questions and nothing else, because near-miss questions retrieve on-topic documents at ordinary distances.

The bridge to part 4, one statement slide: a near-miss is decided by reading the document, and the graph makes that decision explicit. Retrieval returns on-topic text and leaves it to the model to judge whether the specific fact is in it; no prompt-side method reaches that judgement. In the graph the question becomes a typed query and a missing fact is a value. Illustrate with "Kan ik in termijnen betalen?": answered by all ten models in every condition from the Billink document; in the graph, no triple about instalments, verdict unknown, no answer generated. Two things not done, said in one sentence: the comparative-prompt ablation and a hand-labelled sample of the judge.

## 4. Knowledge graph as knowledge base (5 min)

The idea: convert the textual knowledge base into a graph, with as little human work as possible, and decide the outcome of a question (entailed, contradicted, unknown) before any text is generated. Two expected gains, formal reasoning over facts and coverage through inference, and one side benefit: absence becomes a value, so no conversation context can talk the model into an answer.

One slide, three triples. `schema.ttl` is one page: nineteen classes and five property-chain axioms (sells propagates up the category tree, doesNotSell and appliesTo down it, shipsTo down partOf, locatedIn up), plus `completeFor`, a per-subject, per-property switch that closes the world where a document says a list is exhaustive. 39 of the 97 documents are converted by hand into 283 triples, one named graph per document, so every answer cites its source. Show `data/assortiment_19.ttl`:

```turtle
d:DeRodeWinkel drw:sells d:Clothing , d:Accessories ;
    drw:doesNotSell d:Shoes ;
    drw:completeFor drw:sells .
```

Say it plainly: nobody enumerates what a shop does not sell. RDF is open-world, so a missing triple means "not stated"; a "nee" needs either a completeness assertion or an explicit negative. The build validates with SHACL before and after the OWL RL closure (475 derived triples) and reports contradictions between documents with both sources; have the saved output of the conflict test ready as a screenshot instead of running it live, unless time is left.

Then the browser page (`kg_web.py`, started before the talk, http://127.0.0.1:8765; `demo.py` in the terminal is the fallback). Five questions, in this order, about 30 seconds each:

1. Verkopen jullie sneakers? Contradicted, inferred: doesNotSell Shoes propagates down; no triple mentions sneakers.
2. Verkopen jullie wasmachines? Contradicted by completeness; no negative triple for white goods exists anywhere. This is the grounded "nee" that no retrieved chunk can express, and gemma3:12b abstained on it in the study.
3. Verkopen jullie hoodies? Unknown: clothing is sold, the list is "onder andere", hoodies are not listed. The text bot's hedge in typed form.
4. Repareren jullie een Nudie die ik elders heb gekocht? Entailed through the category tree plus an origin rule on the Nudie line.
5. Repareren jullie een Levi's die ik elders heb gekocht? Contradicted: same service, default purchase condition, no rule for Levi's.

Skip 1 if running late; questions 4 and 5 together are the reasoning argument. Each question is mapped to its template by hand, and say so before anyone asks: entity linking from the question text is next step one, so do not take questions from the audience into the graph.

Coverage on the same suite, under the hand mapping: all 68 answerable cases typed, 39 of 40 near-miss cases unknown, all 16 out-of-scope unknown, against 17.5% near-miss false answers for gemma3:12b at k = 0. State the caveat with the number: the mapping was written with the graph in view, so the near-miss column is an upper bound until linking is automatic; "in termijnen" against Billink will tempt a linker exactly as it tempts the models. What the numbers do support: in the graph, abstention is the default and needs no model judgement, a false answer can only come from a linking error, and linking errors can be counted and fixed per label.

Where the graph stops, in two sentences: procedural and explanatory answers (how to redeem a gift card) do not reduce to triples, and the nine templates are a closed set that returns unknown on any new question shape. So the design is graph first, retrieval as fallback, gated on the verdict: contradicted answers "nee" from the evidence, entailed verbalises the evidence, unknown with linked entities falls through to retrieval, unknown with nothing linked abstains without a model call.
