# Knowledge graph as knowledge base

Started 2026-09-21. This directory holds the first artefacts of the second research line on the prototype: converting the textual knowledge base in `docs/` into a knowledge graph with as little human intervention as possible, and answering customer questions from that graph with a three-valued outcome (entailed, contradicted, unknown) decided before any text is generated. The line is independent of the contrastive-examples study in `../contrastive/`; the two share only the 124-case suite.

## What exists

`schema.ttl` is a hand-written OWL schema of about one page: 21 classes (shop, channel, product category, brand, payment method, service, origin rule, opening hours, region, and so on), 32 object properties and 34 datatype properties, and SHACL shapes for validating extracted data. Five property-chain axioms carry the reasoning that the text bot cannot do: `sells` propagates up the category tree, `doesNotSell` and `appliesTo` propagate down it, `shipsTo` propagates down `partOf` between regions and `locatedIn` propagates up it. A `drw:completeFor` triple on a (subject, property) pair switches that pair to closed-world, which is what turns an empty result into a grounded "nee" instead of "geen informatie".

`vocab.ttl` is the controlled vocabulary: the category tree, brands, payment methods, services, days, regions and channels as individuals, each with a canonical Dutch label and `skos:altLabel` entries in the customer's wording ("spijkerbroek", "achteraf betalen", "koopavond", "pinnen").

`data/` holds 63 documents converted to triples by hand, one file per source document, plus one curated file (`curated_store_payment.ttl`, a single explicit negative that the FAQ lacks: the store does not take installment payment), 399 triples in total. The first 39 were converted in September 2026 and the other 24 on 2026-11-10; the section on the nine flows below describes how the later ones differ. Each file is loaded into its own named graph, so every asserted triple carries the document it came from. The documents were chosen to cover the answerable stratum of the suite; 34 of the 97 documents are not converted yet.

`build.py` loads schema, vocabulary and documents, validates the union against the SHACL shapes, computes the OWL RL closure with owlrl and stores the 513 derived triples in a separate `inferred` graph, and writes `graph.trig`. The inferred graph holds domain facts only: OWL and RDFS bookkeeping that the closure also produces (a class being an `rdfs:Class`, a datatype property being an `rdf:Property`) is filtered out, so the count depends only on the schema, vocabulary and documents. pyshacl writes into the graphs it is given, so validation always runs on copies; an earlier version passed the live schema graph and the injected vocabulary triples inflated the count by 53. The SHACL step caught a real modelling conflict on the first run (the physical store node being both a closed `Channel` and a `Store`), which is the kind of check an LLM extraction step will need.

`query.py` implements ten query templates (sells, carries, accepts, offers, repairs, open_on, ships_to, store_in, offers_fit, fact) that return a verdict with supporting triples and their source graphs. A verdict is entailed when the triple exists or is derived, contradicted when an explicit negative exists or the relevant list is declared complete, and unknown otherwise. When the channel of a payment question is unspecified the template asks both channels and combines them, so "Accepteren jullie Bancontact?" comes out unknown with the note that online the list is complete and in store nothing is known.

`suite_map.yml` maps every suite question by hand to a template and its arguments, standing in for the entity linking and routing the runtime bot will have to do. `coverage.py` runs the mapping and writes `coverage.md` with the verdict per case next to the text bot's k0 majority outcome from the study.

`link.py` (added 2026-11-10) is the first automatic replacement for that hand mapping on the entity side. It indexes every `rdfs:label` and `skos:altLabel` in the built dataset, including labels asserted by documents, and resolves a phrase to an individual in three passes: the whole phrase equals a label, the longest label that occurs inside the phrase, and both again on tokens with a Dutch plural ending removed. A class restriction from the caller settles most ambiguity ("cadeaukaart" is a payment method and a service), and a phrase that still matches several individuals returns nothing, so the caller asks instead of guessing. The bot integration below uses it; `coverage.py` does not yet.

## Coverage result (2026-09-21)

On the answerable stratum all 68 cases receive a typed answer: 56 entailed and 12 contradicted, the latter being the six negative-answer questions in both paraphrases (Klarna, VVV-bon online, vestiging Amsterdam, schoenen, wasmachines, laptops). Six of the 68 need inference: the shipping questions about Belgium and Antwerp (Europe covers Belgium covers Antwerp), the sneakers paraphrase of the shoes question (not selling shoes entails not selling sneakers), and the Nudie repair question (repair is stated for clothing and reaches the Nudie brand line through the tree; the origin exception on that line does the rest). The text bot at k0 answered 62 of these 68 and abstained on 6, three of them negative-answer cases the graph settles by completeness.

On the near-miss stratum 38 of 40 cases come out unknown and two contradicted, each a grounded "nee" where the suite expects an abstention. "Verkopen jullie wasmiddel?" falls outside clothing and accessories, which the assortment document declares to be the whole assortment. "Kan ik in termijnen betalen?" is contradicted online by the complete webshop list and in the store by the curated negative in `data/curated_store_payment.ttl`; without that one triple the store side is unknown and so is the verdict, because a channel-unspecified question is only contradicted when every channel is. The text bot answered 7 of these 40 and hedged on 2. All 16 out-of-scope questions are unknown because nothing in them links to the graph.

These numbers are an upper bound. The mapping from question to template and entities was written by hand with the graph in view, so they measure what the graph can express, and say nothing yet about how often an automatic linker would pick the right template and entity. The four inference cases are also a small count; the suite was not written to exercise reasoning, and a v2 suite with compositional questions ("repareren jullie ook een Levi's van een andere winkel?") is where the reasoning claim has to be tested.

## Repair reasoning (added 2026-09-21, reworked the same day)

The category tree has a brand-line level under jeans (`d:LevisJeans`, `d:NudieJeansJeans`, `d:GStarJeans`, and so on), each linked to its brand with `drw:ofBrand`. It is the same shape as "Levi's is a subclass of Jeans is a subclass of Trousers is a subclass of Clothing"; the categories are individuals with `subcategoryOf` rather than OWL classes because the reasoning runs through property chains, and OWL 2 chains can only be built from object properties.

There is one repair service and one alteration service, both instances of `drw:AtelierService`, and both stated once for clothing; the downward `appliesTo` chain carries that to every category under clothing, including the brand lines. Each service has a default `purchaseCondition` (bought at De Rode Winkel). Exceptions are `drw:OriginRule` nodes: `d:NudieRepairException` says that for `RepairService` on the Nudie brand line the origin may be anything, which is the sentence "herstellen ook Nudie jeans die elders zijn gekocht". The `repairs` template resolves a brand to its brand lines, finds the services that apply to the category, and takes the most specific origin rule on the category path, falling back to the service default. Nothing is derived at brand level; the earlier `appliesToBrand` chain was removed because it blurred "applies to Levi's" with "applies to every Levi's line we have".

`extra_questions.yml` holds twelve repair questions outside the 2026-09-14 suite, run with `coverage.py --extra`. All twelve come out as the documents support: a Levi's is repairable (entailed, with the note that this holds for items bought here), a Levi's bought elsewhere is not (contradicted), a Nudie bought elsewhere is (entailed through the exception rule), shoes are not (contradicted, since they are never sold here), and bags or laptops are unknown because repair is stated for clothing only. Nine of the twelve verdicts rest on derived triples.

The first version of this modelling had a third service for Nudie repairs that was asserted to apply to all jeans, and the closure then covered a Levi's bought elsewhere. The rule form makes that mistake harder to write: an exception names one category and one origin, and the SHACL shape for `OriginRule` requires exactly one of each.

## Open world by default, closed world where a document says so (added 2026-09-24)

RDF is open-world: a triple that is absent means "not stated", never "false". The graph keeps that default on purpose, because most lists in the FAQ are partial ("onder andere") and the honest answer to an unlisted item is "geen informatie". A "nee" therefore always needs a positive fact in the graph that licenses it, and there are exactly two such facts. `drw:completeFor` on a (subject, property) pair closes the world for that pair: the listed values are all the values, so an unlisted one is contradicted. `drw:doesNotSell`, `drw:doesNotAccept` and `drw:doesNotOffer` state a single negative that the text spells out. The query templates check them in a fixed order: a positive hit is entailed, an explicit negative is contradicted, an empty result under a completeness assertion is contradicted, and an empty result with neither is unknown. SPARQL itself only answers "triples" or "no triples"; the third value comes from the cascade in `query.py`.

The consequence for whoever converts documents is that negatives are almost never written. The converter writes the positive list, adds `completeFor` when the sentence is exhaustive, and adds a negative predicate only for a sentence of the form "we do not sell X". Nobody enumerates what the shop does not sell. The whole "does not sell" side of the assortment rests on `assortiment_19.ttl`, which asserts `sells Clothing, Accessories`, `doesNotSell Shoes` and `completeFor sells`. Washing machines, electronics, care products and any top-level category added later are contradicted by the completeness assertion alone; the `sells` template walks up to the category directly under `d:Product` and finds it outside the complete list. Hoodies and swim shorts are unknown, because clothing is sold and the clothing list is open. Sneakers, boots and sandals are contradicted through the downward `doesNotSell` chain from shoes, with no triple of their own.

The shoes triple is the model for when an explicit negative is worth writing. For the verdict it is redundant here: shoes is a top-level category, the top-level list is complete, and removing the triple leaves every shoe question contradicted. It is kept for two reasons. The evidence then cites the sentence in the document that says shoes are not sold, which is what the verbaliser needs, and the after-closure SHACL shape raises a conflict the moment another document asserts that some kind of shoe is sold, naming both documents. The negative becomes necessary when the "no" sits inside a category that is sold and whose list is open: "we sell clothing, among other things, but no swim shorts" cannot be expressed by completeness at any level and needs `doesNotSell SwimShorts`, placed on the highest category the sentence covers so that the chain carries it down. The same rule holds for payments and services: `Webshop doesNotAccept Klarna` is written because the FAQ says so, and Bancontact in the store is unknown because the store list is open and no sentence rules it out.

A global closed-world assumption per property would remove most `completeFor` triples and is the wrong trade. It would turn every "onder andere" list into a source of false "nee" answers, and the brand list, the clothing list and the store payment list are all of that kind. Closing the world per (subject, property) costs one triple per exhaustive sentence, which is what the text supports and nothing more.

## How an ontologist would design this (added 2026-09-24)

An ontologist working on a prototype of this size would follow a lightweight methodology such as Linked Open Terms or the NeOn guidelines, and the first artefact would be a list of competency questions: the concrete questions the graph must be able to answer, written in the language of the users. Everything else is judged against that list. The 2026-09-14 suite and `extra_questions.yml` play this role here, and the nine templates in `query.py` are the question shapes the competency questions reduce to. Turning competency questions into executable tests, as `test_kg.py` does, is the recommended way to keep the ontology honest while it changes.

The second step is a search for vocabularies to reuse before minting anything. For a retail FAQ the candidates are schema.org (`Store`, `Product`, `OpeningHoursSpecification`, `paymentAccepted`, `brand`, `areaServed`), GoodRelations for offers and delivery, SKOS for the category tree and its Dutch surface forms, and PROV-O for provenance. The usual practice is to align custom terms to these with `rdfs:subPropertyOf` or `owl:equivalentProperty` so that the data stays interoperable while keeping the semantics the application needs. The prototype mints its own `drw:` terms and uses SKOS only for labels. That is a defensible shortcut for a prototype, since none of the candidate vocabularies express negation, completeness or property chains, and the alignment can be added later without touching the data. An ontologist would probably add the schema.org alignment and a short `rdfs:comment` on every term at the point where the ontology is shared with anyone else.

The third element is the separation of layers that the prototype already has. `schema.ttl` is the ontology proper, the TBox: classes, properties, axioms and shapes. `vocab.ttl` is reference data: the category tree, brands, regions, days and payment methods, which an ontologist would call a SKOS concept scheme. The documents under `data/` are the facts, the ABox. Keeping these in three files with three change rates is standard, because the ontology changes rarely, the reference data changes when the assortment changes, and the facts change with every document.

Modelling the category tree as individuals linked by a transitive `subcategoryOf` is the SKOS pattern for taxonomies and the choice most ontologists would make here, since the categories are things the shop talks about and the reasoning runs through property chains, which OWL 2 only permits over object properties. The alternative, categories as OWL classes with `rdfs:subClassOf`, is common in product classification standards such as GS1 GPC and would be chosen if products were to be instances of their categories. The prototype has no product instances, so the class reading buys nothing.

Negation and completeness are where the design departs most visibly from textbook OWL, and the departure is standard practice. OWL is open-world and monotonic by design, and it offers `owl:NegativePropertyAssertion` for individual negatives, which is reified, does not participate in property chains, and is only used by reasoners for consistency checking. The pragmatic industry pattern is what the prototype does: a named negative property with `owl:propertyDisjointWith` so the reasoner still detects conflicts, and a chain axiom so the negative propagates. For completeness the literature has an exact counterpart. Completeness statements about RDF data sources (Darari, Nutt, Razniewski and others, ISWC 2013) attach a statement "all values of this pattern are present" to a graph and use it to decide when a query answer is complete; `drw:completeFor` is a per-subject, per-property version of the same idea. Rule mining over knowledge bases uses the related partial completeness assumption (AMIE, Galárraga and others, 2013): if a subject has any value for a property, all its values are assumed known. The prototype makes that assumption explicit per subject and property, because the FAQ marks some lists as partial and some as exhaustive.

Exceptions such as the Nudie repair rule are a second place where OWL alone is insufficient, since OWL has no defaults and no non-monotonic reasoning. The standard answer is the n-ary relation pattern from the W3C note on that subject: reify the rule as a node with its parameters, and resolve the most specific rule at query time or with a rule language. `drw:OriginRule` is this pattern, and the SHACL shape that requires exactly one service and one category per rule is how an ontologist would keep the reification well formed.

The placement of reasoning follows a well known split. OWL is for inference, SHACL is for validation, and the two are kept apart because a closed shape that is right on asserted data is wrong on the closure. The prototype materialises the OWL 2 RL closure at build time into its own named graph, which is what triple stores such as GraphDB and RDFox do for read-heavy data, and it chooses the RL profile because it is rule based, tractable and supports the chain axioms. Named graphs per source document are the practical form of provenance; a fuller design would attach PROV-O terms to each graph, and nanopublications are the formalisation of "one graph per assertion with its provenance" for cases where that granularity matters.

Two things an ontologist would add before the graph left the prototype stage are a URI policy and ontology metadata. The IRIs under `derodewinkel.nl/kg/` are fine as a base, and a decision on hash versus slash IRIs, versioning with `owl:versionInfo` and dated version IRIs, and Dublin Core metadata on the ontology header would come before anyone else depended on the terms. Time is the remaining modelling gap: holiday opening hours are handled with a flag, and a fuller model would give opening-hours nodes validity intervals, as `schema:OpeningHoursSpecification` does with `validFrom` and `validThrough`. For a graph of this size the flag is the right amount of ontology, and the principle behind that judgement is Gruber's minimal ontological commitment: state only what the competency questions need, and leave the rest open.

## Validation in two passes (added 2026-09-21)

`build.py` validates twice. The first pass runs the cardinality, datatype, pattern and closed shapes on asserted data only, because the closure adds correct triples (types, derived `sells` on every ancestor, `locatedIn` up the region tree) that those shapes would report. The second pass runs the shapes tagged `drw:phase drw:afterClosure` on asserted plus inferred data: the SPARQL conflict shapes for `sells`/`doesNotSell`, `accepts`/`doesNotAccept` and `offers`/`doesNotOffer`. These catch contradictions that only exist once the chains have fired, and the report names the asserted triples on both sides with their documents, which is what a reviewer needs to decide which document is wrong.

A third check is not SHACL, because it needs the named graphs: for every `drw:completeFor` assertion in document A, any value that another document B asserts for the same subject and property, and that is not in A's list, is reported as a completeness clash. For `sells` the comparison is at the top level of the category tree, matching the query semantics; opening-hours nodes compare by day.

Two files under `tests/` prove the checks fire and pass 1 stays silent. `conflict_after_closure.ttl` asserts `doesNotSell Jeans` and `Webshop doesNotAccept PayPal`; pass 1 conforms, pass 2 reports three conflicts, for example "sells Jeans [doc:assortiment_01], sells KidsJeans [doc:assortiment_18] <-> doesNotSell Jeans [doc:conflict_after_closure]". `conflict_completeness.ttl` asserts a sold detergent, Bancontact online and shipping to the USA; the completeness check reports all three against the documents that declared the lists complete. Run them with `build.py --no-write --extra-data tests/conflict_after_closure.ttl`; the exit code is non-zero when anything is reported, so the build can gate an extraction run.

## RAG and the graph together (added 2026-09-24)

The two research lines were run on the same 124-case suite, so their abstention behaviour can be put side by side. The table gives the graph under the hand-written mapping and the three best-behaved answer models from the contrastive study at k = 0, taken from `../contrastive/RESULTS.md`. A false abstention is an answerable question declined; a false answer is a near-miss or out-of-scope question answered outright.

| system | A false-abstain | N false-answer | O false-answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| graph, hand mapping | 0 of 68 | 2 of 40 (a grounded "nee" on wasmiddel and in termijnen) | 0 of 16 |
| qwen3.5:27b, k0 | 2.9% | 10.0% | 0.0% |
| mistral-small3.2, k0 | 13.2% | 2.5% | 0.0% |
| gemma3:12b, k0 | 8.8% | 17.5% | 0.0% |

The contrast conditions of that study moved every model's threshold towards answering and improved no near-miss rate significantly, so the graph row dominates every RAG row on this suite. Two caveats keep this from being a fair measurement. The abstention decision in the graph run was made by the person who wrote `suite_map.yml` with the graph in view: every near-miss case received either no template or an entity that the graph lacks. Once entity linking is automatic, the decision moves to the linker, and the near-miss questions that fool every answer model ("Kan ik in termijnen betalen?" against the Billink documents, "lengtemaat 38" against the sizing advice) are on-topic questions that an embedding linker can equally map onto a nearby node. The near-miss column is therefore an upper bound until step 1 of the next steps has run. The out-of-scope column is different in kind: nothing in those questions links to any node, so the unknown verdict there is structural and will survive automatic linking.

The claim the numbers do support is qualitative. In the graph, abstention is the default outcome and needs no model judgement; a false answer can only arise from a linking error, and a linking error is a countable, inspectable event that can be fixed per entity or per label. In RAG the same decision is made by the answer model reading a retrieved chunk and judging whether the specific fact is in it, and the contrastive study showed that this judgement shifts with anything that changes the prompt, including the number of documents. A second thing only the graph provides is a grounded negative. "Verkopen jullie wasmachines?", "Kan ik online met Klarna betalen?" and "Hebben jullie een winkel in Amsterdam?" come out contradicted with the completeness or negative triple as evidence, where the answer models either abstained or answered from general text.

The graph cannot replace retrieval in this setting, for four reasons that are all visible in the current data, and they are of two kinds. The first two shrink with work. Coverage is 39 of 97 documents, converted by hand; converting the rest, and extending the ontology where new documents need new terms, is ordinary maintenance and removes this limit. The remaining documents are planned to go through LLM extraction, and completeness assertions are the part of the schema least likely to come out of extraction correctly; a hallucinated `completeFor` yields a confident false "nee", which is a worse failure than the hedged answers the RAG models give on the same questions. That risk is tied to the extraction route and disappears when a person reviews the completeness assertions, of which there are eleven in the current 39 documents. The other two reasons are properties of the approach. Several answerable questions already sit at the edge of what triples express: the sustainability question is a single text literal, the age of the business needs arithmetic on `foundedOn`, and the warranty question leaves the comparison of three months against six to the answer model. Procedural questions such as redeeming a gift card online or deleting an account are step lists, and storing those as literals reproduces retrieval with extra steps; more graphs do not change this, because it is a property of the content type. The nine templates are also a closed set: any question shape outside them returns unknown, which counts as correct on this suite and is a false abstention on any real question of a new shape, where retrieval degrades gracefully. The answer model stays in the loop in every case, because a verdict with evidence still has to become a Dutch sentence.

The design that follows is graph first with retrieval as the fallback, and the verdict gates what happens next. The linker runs on the question and returns zero or more entities and a template. The steps are then:

1. Contradicted: the answer is "nee", verbalised from the evidence triples with the source document as citation. Retrieval is skipped, because the negative rests on a completeness or negative assertion that no retrieved chunk can express.
2. Entailed: the evidence triples are verbalised, and the source documents behind them can be passed to the answer model as context so that the answer keeps the wording and the surrounding detail of the FAQ.
3. Unknown with linked entities: the question is on a topic the graph knows but holds no fact for. Retrieval runs on the original question, and the answer model decides as it does today. The graph's note ("parent category is sold, this one is not listed") can be passed along as an instruction to say so if the documents do not settle it.
4. Unknown with nothing linked: abstain without calling the answer model. This is the out-of-scope stratum, and it costs one linker call.

Under this design the graph handles the templated question shapes, which are yes/no questions about assortment, brands, payment per channel, services, opening hours, shipping and stores, plus single numeric or boolean facts, and retrieval handles procedural and explanatory questions and every question the linker cannot route. The failure modes are separated in the same way. A wrong graph answer is a linking or extraction error, traceable to a label or a document. A wrong retrieval answer is the model extrapolating from on-topic text, which the contrastive study found on the same cases in every model and prompt.

The cheapest test of the design is step 3 of the next steps: a verbaliser that turns a verdict's evidence into pseudo-documents, and a custom retriever that runs the templates and falls through to the existing retriever on unknown. The answer prompt, the suite runner and the judge pipeline then stay as they were in the contrastive study, and the comparison above can be re-run with automatic linking in place of the hand mapping.

## The graph in the bot (added 2026-11-10)

The bot now runs five of the nine templates itself, through ordinary Rasa CALM flows, and the hand-written question-to-template mapping is no longer in the answer path. `data/flows.yml` has one flow per template (`kg_assortiment`, `kg_betaalmethode`, `kg_bezorging`, `kg_openingstijden`, `kg_reparatie`), each with a collect step per template argument. The command generator (gemma3:4b, prompt in `prompts/command_generator.jinja2`) decides whether a question has one of the five shapes, starts the flow and fills the slots with the customer's own words. The optional arguments, the channel of a payment question and the purchase origin of a repair question, are categorical slots with an initial value, so the flow never asks for them and the generator sets them only when the customer mentioned them. The custom actions in `actions/kg_actions.py` build the graph once per process, link each slot value with `link.py` under the class the template expects, run the template, and utter a Dutch sentence that follows from the verdict with the source documents in brackets. The verdict lands in the `kg_verdict` slot, and on unknown, or when a word links to nothing, the flow hands the question to the retrieval path that answered everything before (`action_trigger_search`). Questions outside the five shapes go to retrieval directly, as before. The answer model is not involved in a graph answer: the sentence is a template, so the decision and the wording are both traceable.

`tests/test_kg_flows.yml` asks the bot the eleven demo questions from `demo.py` and one retrieval question, and asserts the flow, the slot values the generator extracted, the verdict and the opening of the answer. On the first run all thirteen passed, including the two cases where the generator has to set an optional slot ("online" for the Klarna question, "elders" for the two repair questions). In a further manual pass "Verzendt u ook naar Belgie?" was routed and linked (the generator wrote "België" with the accent, the linker strips it), "Hoi, hebben jullie koopavond?" reached Thursday through the label a document asserts, and "Hoe gebruik ik mijn cadeaubon online?" stayed with retrieval.

Three things came out of that pass that the next iteration should take up. First, the retrieval answer that follows a fallback sees the graph's own sentence in the conversation context and repeats it, so the fall-through should either be silent or the search prompt should be told to ignore it. Second, when a question of the right shape names no entity ("ik wil weten of jullie iets verkopen"), the generator fills the slot with "iets" rather than leaving it empty, so the collect step never gets to ask; the prompt needs a rule for that case. Third, a question that mentions a product but asks something else about it ("Zijn jullie spijkerbroeken een beetje milieuvriendelijk?") is routed to the assortment flow and answered with "ja, jeans verkopen we", which is the routing error the README predicted above: the graph answers the question it can parse, and the generator has to be stricter about the question shape. The retrieval stress suite shows the same effect from the other side, since three of its cases are now answered by the graph and its assertions expect a retrieval answer; those cases need a graph-aware expectation rather than a change to the bot.

## Costs and benefits of the graph next to retrieval (added 2026-11-10)

The graph is an addition to the retrieval bot, and the design above lets a designer decide how far to push it. With the flows in place the graph answers the question shapes it has templates for and retrieval answers the rest. A designer who needs every answer to be traceable and debuggable can go further and route every question through the graph, with abstention instead of retrieval on unknown, at the price of the coverage ceiling described below. The two research lines together give the material for that decision. The shop is the instance here, and nothing in the trade-off depends on it: any organization that wants to answer questions from its own knowledge, a company, a public service or an association, faces the same costs and gets the same benefits.

The up-front cost is the ontology. Someone has to write the schema, the vocabulary with the wording the users of the assistant actually use, the competency questions and the shapes. That takes an ontology engineer together with domain experts from the organization, the people who know what it offers and under which conditions, where retrieval needs only the documents and an embedding model. The cost continues after the start: the schema has to follow the organization as it changes, a schema change can invalidate data and templates, the vocabulary has to be kept in step with how users talk, and the templates are a closed set, so a question of a shape nobody anticipated returns unknown where retrieval degrades gracefully. Some content does not fit triples at all, such as procedural answers and free-text notes, and arithmetic or comparison is left to whoever verbalizes the answer. If the remaining documents are converted by an LLM, a hallucinated completeness assertion produces a confident false "no", which is a worse failure than the hedged answers retrieval gives on the same questions, so completeness assertions need a person's review. Entity linking is a new place where things go wrong, since the user's words have to be mapped onto the controlled vocabulary before any template can run.

The benefits are the properties the contrastive study could not buy with prompting. The knowledge base can be shown to be internally consistent: the SHACL passes and the completeness check report every conflict at build time, naming the two documents that disagree. A wrong answer can be traced to a triple, a label or a document and fixed there, where a wrong retrieval answer is a model judgement that changes with the prompt, the number of documents and the model version. The graph gives a grounded negative, which retrieval only has when a document spells the negative out, and it reasons over chains that no retrieved chunk expresses, from Europe to Antwerp or from shoes to sneakers. Abstention is the default outcome and needs no model judgement, so the false-answer rate on near-miss questions stops depending on the prompt; the remaining error is a linking error, which is countable and inspectable. Maintaining a fact is one triple, and the answer path can run without a language model, so it is cheap per query, works offline and can be audited. Both failure modes of the combined design are therefore of a known kind: a wrong graph answer is a linking or extraction error, and a wrong retrieval answer is the model extrapolating from on-topic text.

## Nine flows and verbatim answers (added 2026-11-11)

The bot now has nine graph flows. The four added on 2026-11-10 are `kg_merk` (does the shop carry a brand), `kg_dienst` (does a service exist and how does it work), `kg_feit` (one fact about the shop, from a closed list of nineteen such as the address, the shipping fee, the return window and the warranty period) and `kg_jeansmodel` (does the shop have a jeans model or fit, optionally for women or men). The schema grew by fit models, announcement channels, a postal code and a few fact properties, the vocabulary by the matching individuals and labels, and `query.py` by the `offers_fit` template and a guard that returns unknown for an entity outside the vocabulary.

The 24 documents converted for these flows carry the FAQ answer verbatim. Each file states the service or fact as triples and adds the full answer text of the source document as one `drw:howTo` literal, and the bot utters that literal as it stands when the verdict is entailed. This was a deliberate choice: procedural answers do not reduce to triples, and uttering the reviewed text keeps the answer free of model wording. A rephraser that adapts the literal to the customer's register and question is planned as a switch, so that its effect can be measured as an experiment of its own. For these 24 documents the graph works as a lookup from a linked service to a reviewed answer, and the reasoning claims of this line rest on the other 39.

The command prompt lists all nine shapes, with rules for the boundaries between them (brand and repair, brand and jeans model, service and fact, delivery place and delivery fact) and 28 examples. It renders to about 2,500 tokens. At `num_ctx` 4096 gemma3:4b degenerates on it and repeats a single token, which is reproducible outside Rasa, so the command model group in `endpoints.yml` runs at 6144; the other model groups stay at 4096.

`tests/test_kg_flows.yml` has 35 cases. On 2026-11-11, with the server's GPUs free, 33 passed and no model failed to load. Both failures come from the command generator and are marked in the test file. "Ik wil weten of jullie iets verkopen" puts "iets" in the product slot although the prompt forbids it, so the collect question is never asked and the turn falls through to retrieval. "Mijn pakket is beschadigd aangekomen, wat nu?" goes to retrieval directly, because every service example in the prompt asks whether something exists or how it works; retrieval then gives the same content as the graph literal in looser wording. Neither was fixed before the line was parked. The proposed fixes are a guard in the action that resets vague slot values, and one prompt example for a delivery problem.

Eight of the 35 cases are expected to end in retrieval: three questions that the generator should leave to search, and five where a graph flow starts and returns unknown or links nothing. All eight passed. Their assertions check the route and the verdict slot, and they do not check the retrieval answer itself; that is the task of the retrieval stress suite.

The retrieval stress suite (`tests/test_retrieval_stress.yml`, fifteen questions worded differently from the knowledge base) shows how much of the retrieval traffic the four new flows take over. On 2026-11-11 six more of its questions were answered by a graph flow, in addition to the four of 2026-11-10. Four of the six receive a short, correct fact: the return fee, the free-shipping threshold, that an account is optional, and the founding date, the last one uttered as a raw ISO date where the customer asked how old the shop is. Those four cases now assert the flow, the verdict and the sentence. The other two are regressions against retrieval and are marked as known failures in the suite. "Krijg ik korting als student?" starts the service flow and receives the terse how-to literal of the September conversion, without a yes and without the ten percent. "Hoeveel korting leveren m'n gespaarde punten eigenlijk op?" asks what the points are worth and receives the explanation of where to redeem them at checkout; the generator also filled the slot with text copied from the prompt's service list. With the rewritten assertions the suite stands at 13 of 15, and the two failures are these regressions. Three judge-noise failures that the suite had since September are gone, two because the graph now answers those questions with a fixed sentence and one because it is the student-discount case above.

## What the bot result shows and what it leaves open (added 2026-11-11)

The result establishes feasibility. Nine question shapes run through the graph inside the bot with deterministic, sourced answers, grounded negatives appear where retrieval hedges or invents, and the fall-through to retrieval works. It does not yet establish that the combination is better than retrieval alone at an acceptable cost, and five things stand between the two claims.

The 35 cases are a development set. They were written together with the prompt, and several test questions are prompt examples word for word, so 33 of 35 measures whether the prompt does what it was tuned to do. The 124-case suite has never run through the bot with the graph switched on; the suite numbers in the comparison table above come from the hand mapping and are an upper bound. The rate of confident answers to the wrong question is unmeasured. Three instances are known, all found without looking for them: on 2026-11-10 the generator rewrote "in termijnen" to "achteraf betalen" and the graph answered "ja", and the two stress-suite regressions above link the right entity and answer a different question about it. The test questions are clean single questions, and the WhatsApp conversations the knowledge base was distilled from are fragmented, context-dependent and often about one customer's order. The conversion cost is unmeasured as well, since all 63 documents were converted by hand, and reducing that human effort is the main goal of this line.

The router is the part of the design that scales worst. In CALM one LLM call per turn chooses the flow and fills its slots from a prompt that lists every flow. In this project the list is written by hand in the prompt and `flow_retrieval` is off in `config.yml`. Each new template adds a description and examples, and can add a boundary rule against each template it resembles, so the prompt grows with the ontology, and the failures above show a 4b model already ignoring rules. Rasa's own remedies are flow retrieval, which renders only the twenty most similar flows, and a stronger or fine-tuned command model. Flow retrieval would change little here, because the project has ten flows and the nine graph flows are near neighbors that would be retrieved together. The remedy that fits this design is a single `kg` flow: the command generator decides only whether a message is a factual question about the shop, the linker finds the entities, and the classes of the linked entities select the template in code. The prompt then stays constant as templates are added, and the distinctions that depend on the verb, such as carrying a brand and repairing one, need a small rule set or classifier with an evaluation of its own.

## Where the line stopped (2026-11-11)

Work on this line was paused on 2026-11-11 with the state described above committed. This section records the plans that were discussed and not carried out, so that the line can be resumed from the repository.

A held-out routing set from the pseudonymized WhatsApp conversations is the most useful next measurement. Two samples of about thirty conversations were read on 2026-11-11. About half of the conversations are started by the shop and contain pickup or order notices. Questions with a graph shape often occur in the middle of a conversation, one question is often spread over several consecutive messages, and a large share of the customer questions concern one specific order and need a person or the order system. Some questions depend on the conversation so far, and taken alone they would receive a confident wrong graph answer. The plan is to sort each conversation on its timestamps, remove the web-form prefix, quoted replies, automatic replies, deleted and image-only messages and duplicates, merge consecutive customer messages into turns, and keep the preceding context with each turn. A random sample of 150 conversations would be split by conversation into 100 for evaluation, never used in prompts or training, and 50 for development. Each turn would be labeled with a route (one of the nine graph flows with its entity, retrieval, customer-specific, no question, or internal) and with flags for context dependence and multiple questions, pre-labeled by a local model and reviewed by a person. The command generator and the linker would then be run on the evaluation turns, without answer generation, to report routing precision and recall per flow, linking accuracy, the number of confident wrong graph answers, and the share of real traffic per route.

Three rules apply to that data. All processing stays on the project's own server with local models. A few conversations between employees remain in the logs; they are to be flagged by structure, labeled internal and removed, and their content is never summarized into a label file. The pseudonymization left residues, among them a first name, a city, a house number and customer, receipt and order numbers, and some tags replaced ordinary words, so the evaluation set needs a second pseudonymization pass before it leaves the server.

Three smaller ideas came out of the same discussion. The shop's employees ask "Heb je de jeans bij ons gekocht?" before they answer a repair question, which is the purchase condition of the repair rule in the graph; the repair flow currently fills that slot with a default, and asking it as a collect step would match the shop's practice. A router ablation with gemma3:12b as the command model costs one configuration change and would separate the effect of model size from the effect of the design. Fine-tuning a small command model on the project's server is feasible in hardware terms (two 24 GB GPUs, enough for LoRA on a 4B model and 4-bit QLoRA up to about 12B, with about 120 GB of free disk on 2026-11-11), and it is only worth doing after the held-out set exists, since training data paraphrased from the 35 development cases would teach the model the test.

The clarifying questions that employees ask are also the starting point of a separate line of work in `../interrogative_agenda/`.

`test_kg.py` builds the graph and asks it 21 questions as SPARQL over the union of all named graphs, the way a runtime endpoint would see it: shipping to Antwerp (derived through partOf), Thursday and koopavond, sneakers (derived doesNotSell), washing machines (top-level completeness), Klarna and Bancontact per channel, repairs on a Levi's bought here or elsewhere and the Nudie exception, evidence provenance, and the two conflict test files. Each SPARQL answer is compared with the verdict of the matching template in `query.py`.

They need `rdflib`, `owlrl` and `pyshacl` in the venv (`pip install rdflib owlrl pyshacl`); the tests use `pytest` when it is installed.
